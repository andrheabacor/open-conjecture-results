# A counterexample to the exhaustiveness conjecture in OEIS A046385

**SOLVED.** A046385 has terms at indices **14, 17 and 21**. Hugo Pfoertner's proposed list omits all three. Lean proves the negation of his full conjecture. The three constructions give upper bounds for the least values at those indices. Whether the sequence has infinitely many terms is a separate question. [OEIS A046385](https://oeis.org/A046385) · [Lean proof](../OpenConjectures/A046385.lean)

## The conjecture and its status

For each nonnegative index $k$, take the smallest positive decimal palindrome whose complete prime factorization has $k$ factors, counting repetitions, with every factor itself a decimal palindrome. The displayed data run from index 0 to 13. A comment supplies three further indices, 15, 16 and 20. Hugo Pfoertner wrote on August 13, 2019, “Conjecture: The sequence is finite and has no other terms than those shown here.” [Official entry record](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/oeis/oeisdata/main/seq/A046/A046385.seq)

I checked the live entry, its table and both relevant pages of the revision history on November 27, 2026. Revision 11 introduces the conjecture and revision 17 approves the 2019 edits. The latest revision shown, 21 on January 24, 2021, follows a program-credit change. The conjecture is still present. The entry links a table of values through index 13 and gives no research paper as a reference. [2019 revision history](https://oeis.org/history?seq=A046385&start=10) · [Latest revisions](https://oeis.org/history?seq=A046385) · [OEIS table](https://oeis.org/A046385/b046385.txt)

Patrick De Geest's own table leaves index 14 open. The related A309565 allows additional nonpalindromic prime factors. A071277 allows composite palindromic factors and records the familiar trick of making two copies of a palindrome by a decimal shift. A046376 records carry-free products in the two-prime case. These sources explain why similar-looking constructions and tables require checking the complete prime factorization here. [De Geest, section 1](https://worldofnumbers.com/assign3.htm) · [A309565](https://oeis.org/A309565) · [A071277](https://oeis.org/A071277) · [A046376](https://oeis.org/A046376)

Searches using the A-number, author, conjecture wording, and variants of “palindromes with only palindromic prime factors” found no earlier resolution. I also searched for the specific multiplier. The closest research result located proves the existence of infinitely many palindromes with at most six prime factors, without requiring those factors to be palindromes. The searches support describing A046385's claim as publicly unresolved before this construction. They cannot establish absolute priority. [Tuxanidy and Panario](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.16637)

## Disproof

Let

```text
p = 10^46 + 10^33 + 10^23 + 10^13 + 1
  = 10000000000001000000000100000000010000000000001.
```

The five nonzero positions are symmetric about position 23, so $p$ is a decimal palindrome. It is prime. For a reproducible primality proof, factor

```text
p - 1 = 2^13 · 5^13 · 17 · 1229 · 5030702572783 · 9514162267188379.
```

With base 6, modular exponentiation gives $6^{p-1}=1$ modulo $p$, while $6^{(p-1)/q}$ differs from 1 modulo $p$ for every prime divisor $q$ in that factorization. Thus 6 has multiplicative order $p-1$ modulo $p$, which forces $p$ to be prime by Lucas's criterion. The Lean file supplies the recursive certificates for the prime divisors and checks the modular powers. [Lucas certificate in the proof](../OpenConjectures/A046385.lean)

Take $s=677707776=2^{16}\cdot 3^3\cdot383$. Its nine digits form a palindrome, and its twenty prime factors are all palindromes. In $sp$, the five shifted copies of $s$ start at positions 0, 13, 23, 33 and 46, counted from the right. The gaps between their starting positions are 13, 10, 10 and 13. There are no carries. Reading left to right, the product consists of five identical nine-digit blocks separated by four zeros, one zero, one zero and four zeros. It is therefore a palindrome.

```text
s · p = 6777077760000677707776067770777606777077760000677707776
      = 2^16 · 3^3 · 383 · p.
```

This factorization has **21** factors counted with multiplicity. Every factor is a palindromic prime. Therefore the set defining $a(21)$ is nonempty and has a least member. The proposed list stops at index 20. This single witness disproves the conjecture.

The same multiplier gives two more counterexamples. All three seeds are palindromes of at most nine digits, so the same symmetric pattern of disjoint copies applies.

| New index | Seed $s$ | Complete prime factorization of $s$ | Certified upper bound for $a(k)$ |
| ---: | ---: | --- | --- |
| 14 | 2977792 | $2^{12}\cdot727$ | $2977792\,p$ |
| 17 | 40955904 | $2^{12}\cdot3^2\cdot11\cdot101$ | $40955904\,p$ |
| 21 | 677707776 | $2^{16}\cdot3^3\cdot383$ | $677707776\,p$ |

## Lean definition and verification

The file uses Mathlib's `Nat.digits 10` and equality with its reverse. Reversing the little-endian order used by Mathlib gives the ordinary decimal digit order and preserves the palindrome test. `Qualifies k n` requires a positive $n$, a palindromic $n$, length $k$ for the complete list `n.primeFactorsList`, and the palindrome property for every member. `Nat.primeFactorsList_unique` connects each displayed factorization to that full list.

The sequence `a : ℕ → Option ℕ` returns `some (Nat.find h)` whenever a qualifying integer exists and `none` otherwise. The theorem `first_terms` checks the exact minima 1, 2, 4, 8, 88 and 252 at indices 0 through 5, using `decide +kernel` both on the candidates and on every smaller integer. The general lemma `term_le_of_witness` proves that a witness forces the least value to exist and gives an upper bound for it.

`displayed` records every value listed by OEIS, including all three values in the comment. `PfoertnerConjecture` asserts both finiteness of the domain and pointwise equality of the entire partial sequence with that table. `pfoertnerConjecture_false` disproves this unrestricted statement at index 21. [Definitions, witnesses and the final theorem](../OpenConjectures/A046385.lean)

Run from the repository root (originally checked in PowerShell with Lean 4.37.0-rc1 and Mathlib revision `31ce06eaadf4a200c0e1407c5f10953cf606b8f8`).
