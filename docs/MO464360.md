# MathOverflow 464360 — A carry-free subsequence

**SOLVED.** The odd integers selected by the recurrence in MathOverflow 464360 form an infinite subsequence of OEIS A110978. Lean 4 checked a strictly increasing map between the indices of the two increasing enumerations. [Formal proof](../OpenConjectures/MO464360.lean)

## The question

On 17 February 2024, MathOverflow user231922 proposed the following claim. [Original question](https://mathoverflow.net/questions/464360/possible-subsequence-of-the-a110978)

> I conjecture that $c(n)$ is a subsequence of $a(n)$.

Here $a$ lists the odd composite integers admitting a factorization into two integers greater than one whose ordinary binary long multiplication needs no carry. For example, $15=3\cdot5$ has binary partial products $11_2$ and $1100_2$. The source's program enumerates proper factors and tests whether any output column receives two set bits. [OEIS A110978](https://oeis.org/A110978) · [MathOverflow definition and program](https://mathoverflow.net/questions/464360/possible-subsequence-of-the-a110978)

To compute $b(n)$, start at $x=n$. While $x<2n$, write $x=2^t d$ with $d$ odd and replace $x$ by $x+\min(2^t,d)$. The returned value is the first state at least $2n$. The sequence $c$ lists in increasing order the odd $k$ satisfying $b((k-1)/2)=k$. For $n=7$, the states are $7,8,9,10,12,15$. [Original recurrence](https://mathoverflow.net/questions/464360/possible-subsequence-of-the-a110978)

## Proof

Take any selected $k=2n+1$. The initial value $n$ is smaller than $k$, so some last active state $x<2n$ leads to $k$. In the decomposition $x=2^t d$, put $s=\min(2^t,d)$. Then
$$
s=2n+1-x\ge 2.
$$
Both $2^t$ and $d$ are therefore at least two. In particular $t>0$ and $x$ is even. If $s=2^t$, the output $x+s$ would also be even. Hence $s=d<2^t$, giving the nontrivial factorization
$$
k=d(2^t+1).
$$
The set bits of $d$ occupy positions $0$ through $t-1$ at most. The multiplier $2^t+1$ has only bits $0$ and $t$ set. Its two nonzero partial products therefore occupy disjoint blocks, so no column produces a carry. This proves membership in A110978 for every selected $k$. [Lean membership theorem](../OpenConjectures/MO464360.lean)

**There are infinitely many selected terms.** For $t\ge2$, put $M=3\cdot2^t$ and $n=M/2+1$, so the stopping threshold is $M+2$. A step from $0<x<M$ cannot pass $M$. To see this, write $2^e$ for the largest power of two dividing $x$. If $e\le t$, both $x$ and $M$ are multiples of $2^e$, so the positive gap is at least $2^e$ and the step is at most $2^e$. If $e>t$, the only positive multiple of $2^{t+1}$ below $M$ is $2^{t+1}$ itself, whose step is one. The increasing walk consequently reaches $M$. Its next step is $\min(2^t,3)=3$, so $b(n)=M+3=2n+1$. This gives $15,27,51,99,\ldots$ in $c$, with no upper bound. [Lean family and infinitude](../OpenConjectures/MO464360.lean)

Since both enumerations are increasing, each $c(i)$ occurs at a unique index $g(i)$ in $a$, and $i<j$ implies $g(i)<g(j)$. Lean proves this for the actual infinite enumerations. [Lean subsequence theorem](../OpenConjectures/MO464360.lean)

| While $x<2n$, advance and stop at the first threshold crossing | `run` stops on the first state meeting the threshold. `b` starts it at $n$ with fuel $n$. Theorems `increment_pos` and `b_reaches` prove this budget always suffices. At $n=0$ the loop stops immediately. |
