# Hamilton's infinite family in OEIS A179697

**Result, 27 November 2026.** Every integer whose binary expansion is `10`, followed by $j>1$ ones, followed by `010`, belongs to A179697. Its value is $6(2^{j+2}-1)$. In particular A179697 is infinite. Lean checks the universal statement, the construction, and unboundedness. [Lean source](../OpenConjectures/A179697.lean)

## Source and open status

Dylan Hamilton's OEIS entry, dated 24 July 2010, states the following conjecture.

> All numbers whose binary representation is of the form 10(j 1s)010, where j>1, appear to be terms of this sequence.

Let $R$ reverse the canonical binary digits of a natural number and interpret the result as a number, so a trailing zero drops out under reversal. Put $s=k+R(k)$ and $d=|k-R(k)|$. The defining equations are $|s-R(s)|=k$ and $d+R(d)=k$. The first entry is 90. The unrestricted equations also hold at zero, and every integer in Hamilton's proposed family is positive. ## Proof

Write $j=t+2$, with $t\ge 0$. Let $q$ be the integer with binary expansion `10`, then $j$ ones, then `01`. This word is a palindrome. Hamilton's integer $k$ is obtained by appending a zero, so $k=2q$ and $R(k)=R(q)=q$.

Write $1^t$ for a run of $t$ ones, including the empty run for $t=0$. Ordinary binary place values give the following three identities.

| Integer | Binary word, most significant digit first | Value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $q$ | `10` · $1^{t+2}$ · `01` | $48\cdot 2^t-3$ |
| $3q$ | `1000` · $1^t$ · `0111` | $144\cdot 2^t-9$ |
| $5q$ | `1110` · $1^t$ · `0001` | $240\cdot 2^t-15$ |

For instance, the middle word has value $8\cdot 2^{t+4}+16(2^t-1)+7$. The last two words are reversals of one another. Consequently $s=k+R(k)=3q$, $R(s)=5q$, and $|s-R(s)|=2q=k$. Also $d=|2q-q|=q$, so $d+R(d)=q+q=k$. This proves both defining equations for every permitted run length.

Finally $k=96\cdot 2^t-6=6(2^{j+2}-1)$. These values increase without bound. For $j=2$, $k=90$, $q=45$, and the reversed pair is $135\leftrightarrow 225$.

## Lean correspondence and verification

`rev` uses Mathlib's `Nat.digits 2`, list reversal, and `Nat.ofDigits 2`. The two truncated differences in `gap a b` add up to the absolute difference in either order. `Equations` contains the two original equalities. `Member` adds positivity. `hamiltonWord j` is the literal concatenation `[1,0] ++ List.replicate j 1 ++ [0,1,0]`, read most significant digit first.

`hamilton_family` quantifies over every natural $k$ and $j>1$ whose canonical word has that form. `hamilton_construction` proves that evaluating each word produces that very canonical word, a positive member, and the stated value. `hamilton_unbounded` produces a member greater than any prescribed bound. The construction verifies the defining equations directly. The checked examples use `by decide` for the first six family values, for several listed memberships including 23130 outside the family, and for the exact members below 400.
